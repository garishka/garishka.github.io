Black Holes Surrounded by a Dark Matter Halo

We consider a spacetime geometry describing a superposition of a black hole and a dark matter halo at large distance. The solution is inspired by the Einstein cluster — a stationary self-gravitating system of many massive particles moving on circular geodesics in the field created by their collective superposition, first described by Einstein and subsequently revisited in the literature. Such a system is spherically symmetric, and the metric takes the general static form where the metric functions and depend only on the radial coordinate .

Linear Polarization for Black Holes with a Dark Matter Halo

This section presents a systematic analysis of the linear polarization of synchrotron emission in the static spacetime of a black hole surrounded by a dark matter halo. The central objective is to quantify how the dark matter content modifies the observable polarization pattern relative to the Schwarzschild case, across different magnetic field configurations and inclination angles. We examine both the intensity and orientation (EVPA) of the polarization vector, with particular emphasis on the role of the halo compactness parameter .

Orbiting Hotspots

Orbiting hot spots are compact, localized regions of enhanced radiation within the accretion disk of a black hole . These features emerge as a result of magnetic reconnection, plasma instabilities, or turbulence in the surrounding environment . In our formulation, the hot spot is treated as a point-like emitter following a circular, counterclockwise, equatorial geodesic orbit.

Polarized Image of an Equatorial Emitting Ring

The goal of this analysis is to describe the light reaching a distant observer from a thin fluid ring orbiting at a fixed radial coordinate in the vicinity of a black hole with a dark matter halo. The fluid emits linearly polarized synchrotron radiation in a locally constant magnetic field, and the observable polarization at large distances is obtained by parallel-transporting the polarization four-vector along null geodesics.
